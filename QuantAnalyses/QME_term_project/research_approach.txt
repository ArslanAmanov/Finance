These three ideas can be combined into a **coherent statistics + modeling research project**, but there is an important distinction between the **research question**, the **Financial Inclusion Score**, and the **Financial/Digital Inclusion Dimension**.

### 1. Main research question

> **What demographic, socioeconomic, and digital-access factors are associated with an individual's use of digital financial services?**

This becomes your **main dependent-variable modeling problem**.

For example:

$$
DigitalFinancialUse_i =
f(Demographics_i,\ Socioeconomic_i,\ DigitalAccess_i)
$$

You could define digital financial use from indicators such as digital payments, mobile money, or digital account usage.

---

## 2. Financial Inclusion Score

This would be your **constructed composite indicator**.

Instead of looking at only one behavior, you combine several dimensions of financial inclusion:

| Component            | Example                             |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Account ownership    | Has an account                      |
| Digital payments     | Makes/receives digital payments     |
| Formal saving        | Saves through financial institution |
| Formal borrowing     | Borrows from financial institution  |
| Mobile money         | Uses mobile money                   |
| Financial resilience | Can access emergency funds          |

You could initially create:

$$
FI_i = \frac{Account_i + Payment_i + Saving_i + Borrowing_i + MobileMoney_i}{5}
$$

giving each individual a score between 0 and 1.

For example:

**Person A**

```text
Account       = 1
Digital Pay   = 1
Saving        = 1
Borrowing     = 0
Mobile Money  = 1
------------------
FI Score      = 0.80
```

**Person B**

```text
Account       = 0
Digital Pay   = 0
Saving        = 1
Borrowing     = 0
Mobile Money  = 0
------------------
FI Score      = 0.20
```

You can then investigate:

> **What explains variation in financial inclusion scores across individuals?**

---

# 3. Financial/Digital Inclusion Dimension

This is where **PCA** becomes useful.

Rather than deciding arbitrarily that every indicator should receive a 20% weight, let the data identify the underlying structure.

Suppose you have:

$$
X =
\begin{bmatrix}
Account &
DigitalPayment &
MobileMoney &
Saving &
Borrowing &
Internet &
Mobile
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Standardize them:

$$
Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}
$$

Then perform:

$$
Z \rightarrow PCA
$$

The first principal component:

$$
PC_1 =
w_1Account+
w_2DigitalPayment+
w_3MobileMoney+
\cdots+
w_7Mobile
$$

could represent a latent:

> **Financial/Digital Inclusion Dimension**

if the loadings support that interpretation.

---

This gives you **two different analytical approaches**.

---

## A. Financial Inclusion Score → measurement

Your question becomes:

> **How financially included is an individual?**

Output:

$$
FI_i \in [0,1]
$$

You can compare:

* countries
* gender
* income groups
* education
* age
* employment
* urban/rural

---

## B. PCA Dimension → latent structure

Your question becomes:

> **Can multiple financial and digital indicators be summarized into an underlying Financial/Digital Inclusion dimension?**

You investigate:

### PCA loadings

For example, hypothetically:

| Variable          | PC1 loading |
| ----------------- | ----------: |
| Digital payment   |        0.48 |
| Account ownership |        0.46 |
| Mobile money      |        0.43 |
| Internet          |        0.41 |
| Formal saving     |        0.38 |
| Formal borrowing  |        0.29 |
| Mobile ownership  |        0.25 |

Then you could say:

> PC1 captures a broad financial/digital inclusion dimension, characterized primarily by digital payment usage, account ownership, mobile money usage, and digital connectivity.

You should **not automatically call PC1 "financial inclusion"** just because it is PC1. You need to examine the loadings, explained variance, and conceptual validity.

---

# Then connect it back to your main research question

This is where the project becomes particularly strong.

You could create:

$$
FI\ Score_i
$$

and/or

$$
PC1_i
$$

and investigate:

> **What demographic, socioeconomic, and digital-access factors are associated with higher financial/digital inclusion?**

For example:

$$
FI_i =
\beta_0+
\beta_1 Female_i+
\beta_2 Age_i+
\beta_3 Education_i+
\beta_4 Income_i+
\beta_5 Employment_i+
\beta_6 Internet_i+
\beta_7 Mobile_i+
\epsilon_i
$$

Depending on how you construct the score, you could use:

* OLS
* fractional logit
* beta regression
* ordered models if you categorize the score

For **digital financial service usage**, use logistic regression:

$$
P(DigitalUse_i=1)
=
Logit^{-1}(X_i\beta)
$$

# I would formulate your project as

### **Title**

> **Determinants of Digital Financial Inclusion: Evidence from the World Bank Global Findex Database**

### Research question

> **What demographic, socioeconomic, and digital-access factors are associated with an individual's use of digital financial services?**

### Research objectives

**Objective 1 — Measurement**

Construct a **Financial Inclusion Score** from multiple financial-service indicators.

**Objective 2 — Dimensionality**

Use **PCA** to identify an underlying **Financial/Digital Inclusion Dimension**.

**Objective 3 — Statistical inference**

Test whether demographic, socioeconomic, and digital-access characteristics are significantly associated with financial inclusion.

**Objective 4 — Predictive modeling**

Develop and compare models predicting whether an individual uses digital financial services.
